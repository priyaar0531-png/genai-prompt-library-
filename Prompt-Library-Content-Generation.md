## Prompt #1: LinkedIn Post for Product Launch

**Techniques used:** Role + Audience Targeting + Constraints

**Template:**
```text
You are a senior B2B content marketer who writes high-engagement LinkedIn posts.

Write a LinkedIn post announcing the launch of {product_name}, a {product_category} built for {target_audience}.

Key benefit to highlight: {key_benefit}
Tone: {tone}
Length: {word_limit} words max
Structure: hook line, 2-3 short paragraphs, one line with a relevant emoji list of features, end with a CTA: {cta_text}
Include 3-5 relevant hashtags at the end.
```

**Output:** To be generated by testing this prompt.

**Iteration:**

- **V1:**
  ```text
  Write a LinkedIn post about {product_name} launching.
  ```
  **Problem with V1:** No role, no audience, no tone/length control, no structure — output would be generic and unusable without heavy editing.

- **V2:** (shown above — Role + Audience Targeting + Constraints)

- **What improved and why:** Adding a Role ("senior B2B content marketer") anchors the writing style and quality bar. Audience Targeting ({target_audience}, {key_benefit}) makes the message relevant instead of generic. Constraints (word limit, structure, CTA) make the output directly postable instead of needing a rewrite.

## Prompt #3: Cold Outreach Email

**Techniques used:** Role + Few-shot + Constraints

**Template:**
```text
You are a sales development rep writing concise, non-pushy cold emails.

Here are examples of the style I want:

Example 1:
Subject: Quick question about {example_pain_point_1}
Hi {{First Name}}, noticed {example_trigger_1}. We help teams like yours {example_benefit_1} — worth a 15-min chat?

Example 2:
Subject: {example_pain_point_2}?
Hi {{First Name}}, saw {example_trigger_2}. Curious if {example_benefit_2} is still a priority for you this quarter.

Now write a cold outreach email to {recipient_role} at {recipient_company}, referencing {trigger_event} as the reason for reaching out, and offering {value_proposition}.
Constraints: under {word_limit} words, one clear CTA ({cta_text}), no generic flattery, subject line included.
```

**Output:** To be generated by testing this prompt.

**Iteration:**

- **V1:**
  ```text
  Write a cold email to {recipient_role} at {recipient_company} about {value_proposition}.
  ```
  **Problem with V1:** No style reference, so tone varies wildly between generations; no length or CTA constraint, leading to long, salesy-sounding emails.

- **V2:** (shown above — Role + Few-shot + Constraints)

- **What improved and why:** Few-shot examples lock in a consistent, low-pressure tone that matches real cold-email best practice. The Role reinforces that framing. Constraints (word limit, single CTA) keep the email skimmable, which is the actual goal of a cold email.

## Prompt #9: Article Summarization

**Techniques used:** Transformation + Constraints

**Template:**
```text
Summarize the following article for {target_audience}. Preserve the key facts and any numbers/statistics; do not add information that isn't in the source.

Article:
{article_text}

Output format:
- One-sentence TL;DR
- {num_bullets} bullet points covering the main points
- Length: total summary under {word_limit} words
- Tone: {tone}
```

**Output:** To be generated by testing this prompt.

**Iteration:**

- **V1:**
  ```text
  Summarize this article: {article_text}
  ```
  **Problem with V1:** No target length, no audience framing, and no output structure — results range from one line to multiple paragraphs, inconsistent for reuse across articles.

- **V2:** (shown above — Transformation + Constraints)

- **What improved and why:** Specifying the output format (TL;DR + fixed bullet count) makes summaries consistent and scannable across different articles. The instruction not to add outside information reduces hallucination risk, and the audience/tone constraint makes the summary usable in different contexts (e.g., internal digest vs. public post) just by changing placeholders.

## Prompt #10: Tone Rewriter (Formal ↔ Casual)

**Techniques used:** Transformation + Few-shot

**Template:**
```text
Rewrite text between formal and casual tone while preserving the original meaning and any factual details.

Examples:
Formal: "We regret to inform you that your request cannot be processed at this time."
Casual: "Hey, quick heads up — we can't process this request right now."

Formal: "Please find attached the requested documentation for your review."
Casual: "Attached is the doc you asked for — take a look when you get a chance!"

Now rewrite the following text from {source_tone} to {target_tone}:

Text: {input_text}

Constraints: keep the same core message and any names/numbers unchanged; length should stay within {percent_length_variance}% of the original.
```
